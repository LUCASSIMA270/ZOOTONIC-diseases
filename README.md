🌳 ZOOTONIC-diseases: Deforestation & Pathogen Spillover
How Forest Disturbance Shapes Zoonotic Disease Risk Across Scales

This repository contains the full analytical pipeline for the transversal project investigating the link between environmental degradation and global health. Using high-resolution satellite data and international health proxies, we examine how forest disturbance (measured through tree cover loss) correlates with the emergence of zoonotic diseases.
🧐 The Research Question

"How is forest disturbance, measured through tree cover loss, associated with zoonotic disease risk across spatial scales and over time?"

The study explores whether aggregating data at the national level masks local spillover dynamics and evaluates the temporal window—how long it takes for an environmental shock to translate into a detectable public health risk.
🛠️ The Data & Methodology

    Data Sources:

        Deforestation: Annual tree cover loss and primary forest loss from Global Forest Watch (GFW).

        Health & Controls: Zoonotic risk proxies and socioeconomic indicators (GDP, population density, urbanization) from the World Bank's World Development Indicators (WDI).

    Econometric Strategy:

        Fixed-Effects Panel Models: Controlling for time-invariant characteristics and global shocks.

        Multi-Scale Comparison: Analyzing results at the global, regional (Asia & Africa), and subnational levels.

        Dynamic Specifications: Testing the impact of deforestation with 1-year and 3-year temporal lags.

📈 Key Findings

    The Aggregation Gap: No statistically significant association is found at the national level, suggesting that macroeconomic data masks local ecological realities.

    Local Significance: At the subnational level, forest disturbance is associated with a modest but significant increase in zoonotic risk.

    Short-Term Impact: The effects are primarily short-term, appearing contemporaneously or with a 1-year lag, before dissipating after 3 years.

    Socioeconomic Modifiers: Factors like urbanization and economic capacity (GDP) play a crucial role in mitigating or exposing populations to these risks.
